The problem

When you follow a topic across several publications, a new article often spends most of its time on familiar ground. A summary tells you what the article says. As a returning reader, I wanted to answer a more specific question: What does this add to what I’ve already read?

How the comparison works

Index passages, query with the full article

When a reader saves an article, the backend extracts its text, applies translation when needed, and groups sentences into passages of about 1,000 characters or less. It stores those passages in ChromaDB with the reader and topic as metadata.

For comparison, a single query uses the full current article. Filtering by reader and topic keeps matches within the chosen reading history. Storing the history in smaller passages lets the API include a relevant excerpt without taking up prompt space with an entire earlier article.

What “new” means

The reference point is the reader’s history. If the backend finds a related passage, it can compare what the two articles actually say. If it finds no matching passage, the narrower conclusion is “I couldn’t find this in your earlier reading.” That distinction matters because retrieval can miss a relevant passage.
